A six-month consulting posting in Doha, Qatar, delivering SAP UI5 Fiori
applications — my third country and the shortest engagement in my career.

_Client work lives in client systems. This is about the practice, not the
deliverables._

It is included here because short postings teach something long ones don't. You
have no accumulated context, no relationships to trade on, and no time to build
either before your first deliverable is due. Everything that would normally be
absorbed through months of proximity has to be made explicit in week one.

Two things followed from that.

The first is that I stopped treating a requirements document as a description
and started treating it as a contract about vocabulary. When the team spans
languages and working cultures, the words in the specification are doing more
work than usual, and the cost of two people meaning different things by the
same term compounds fast. Writing definitions down early felt pedantic and
saved the engagement time repeatedly.

The second is a healthier respect for right-to-left and multi-language layout.
A Fiori layout that has only ever been reviewed in English is not a finished
layout — it is a layout that hasn't met its hardest case yet.

Both habits came back with me and are now how I run vendor specifications as a
technical lead.
